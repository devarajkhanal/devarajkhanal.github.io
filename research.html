Research

Plants, diversity, and environmental adaptation

My research interests center on plant genetic resources, genetic and phenotypic diversity, environmental adaptation, and horticultural and specialty crop improvement.

01
Research Area

Horticulture & Plant Phenotyping

Plant traits · Hyperspectral phenotyping · Botanical collections · Field research

My background in horticulture provides the foundation for my interest in how plants grow, respond to their environments, and express measurable traits. My research experience includes plant trait characterization, hyperspectral leaf scanning, botanical collections, herbarium research, and field-based work with perennial and horticultural crops.

I am interested in using plant phenotypes and observations to better understand differences among plant materials and their relationships with growing environments. This includes both field-based observations and laboratory approaches that provide information about plant traits.

My experience with botanical and herbarium collections has also given me an appreciation for the value of plant specimens and associated geographic information as resources for understanding plant diversity and environmental relationships.

Research experience

Plant trait characterization, hyperspectral leaf scanning, plant and herbarium specimen handling, botanical collection research, field-based horticultural research, perennial crop systems, and plant observation and documentation.

02
Research Area

Crop Environment & Adaptation

Cultivated mango · Climate · Environmental variation · Geographic adaptation

My graduate research at Saint Louis University and the Missouri Botanical Garden focused on the climatic environment of cultivated mango (Mangifera indica L.). I examined environmental conditions associated with cultivated mango across its region of domestication and introduced and cultivated regions.

This work involved examining geographic and environmental variation, including temperature and precipitation patterns, to better understand the climatic conditions associated with cultivated mango.

The project also incorporated extensive literature concerning mango cultivation, domestication, introduction history, geographic origins, and diversity in South and Southeast Asia.

Research focus

Understanding relationships among cultivated crops, geographic distributions, climatic conditions, and environmental variation.

  • Climatic niche characterization
  • Temperature and precipitation patterns
  • Environmental variation
  • Cultivated mango
  • Domestication and cultivation history
  • Introduction and geographic distribution
  • Mango diversity in South and Southeast Asia
03
Research Area

Plant Diversity, Genetics & Evolution

Plant genetic resources · Phylogenetics · Evolution · Molecular research

At South Dakota State University, my research focused on plant diversity and evolutionary relationships within the Russian thistle group (Salsola spp.). This work involved phylogenetic relationships, classification, species identification, and understanding evolutionary patterns within a complex group of plants.

My molecular research experience includes plant specimen organization, voucher records, molecular records and inventories, DNA-related laboratory work, and target-capture sequencing approaches.

I also have experience with Russian thistle identification and management, as well as contribution to DNA laboratory work associated with an ongoing mango phylogeny project.

Research experience

Phylogenetic research, plant diversity and evolution, species identification and classification, plant voucher records, molecular records and inventories, target-capture sequencing approaches, and DNA laboratory work.

  • Salsola phylogenetic research
  • Plant diversity and evolution
  • Species identification and classification
  • Plant voucher records
  • Molecular records and inventories
  • Target-capture sequencing approaches
  • DNA laboratory work
Research Direction

Connecting diversity with environment

My broader research interests are centered on understanding how genetic and phenotypic diversity in horticultural and specialty crops interacts with environmental variation. I am interested in research that can contribute to crop adaptation, improvement, resilience, and sustainable production.

Research

Plants, diversity, and environmental adaptation

My research interests center on plant genetic resources, genetic and phenotypic diversity, environmental adaptation, and horticultural and specialty crop improvement.

01

Horticulture & Plant Phenotyping

Plant traits · Hyperspectral phenotyping · Botanical collections · Field research

My background in horticulture provides the foundation for my interest in understanding plant traits, plant performance, and the relationship between plants and their growing environments.

My research experience includes plant trait characterization, hyperspectral leaf scanning, herbarium and botanical collections, and field-based work with perennial and horticultural crops.

I am particularly interested in approaches that connect observable plant traits with environmental variation and plant genetic resources. These approaches can help characterize diversity and support the evaluation and improvement of horticultural and specialty crops.

Research experience

  • Plant trait characterization
  • Hyperspectral leaf scanning
  • Plant and herbarium specimen handling
  • Botanical collection research
  • Field-based horticultural research
  • Perennial crop systems
  • Plant observation and documentation
02

Crop Environment & Adaptation

Cultivated mango · Climate · Environmental variation · Geographic adaptation

My graduate research at Saint Louis University and the Missouri Botanical Garden focused on the climatic environment of cultivated mango (Mangifera indica L.).

I examined environmental patterns associated with cultivated mango by comparing climatic conditions across its region of domestication and regions where mango has been introduced and cultivated.

This work considers how temperature and precipitation patterns vary across mango-growing regions and how these environmental differences relate to the distribution and cultivation of this important perennial crop.

My research also draws on extensive literature concerning mango cultivation, domestication, introduction history, geographic origins, and diversity across South and Southeast Asia.

Mangifera indica research

  • Climatic niche characterization
  • Temperature and precipitation patterns
  • Environmental variation
  • Cultivated mango
  • Domestication and cultivation history
  • Introduction and geographic distribution
  • South and Southeast Asian diversity
03

Plant Diversity, Genetics & Evolution

Plant genetic resources · Phylogenetics · Evolution · Molecular research

My earlier graduate research at South Dakota State University focused on plant diversity and evolutionary relationships within the Russian thistle group (Salsola spp.).

The work involved examining phylogenetic relationships, classification, and identification within a taxonomically complex group of plants.

I worked with plant specimens, voucher records, molecular records, and target-capture sequencing approaches as part of the research.

My broader experience also includes Russian thistle identification and management, as well as contribution to DNA laboratory work for an ongoing mango phylogeny project.

Research experience

  • Salsola phylogenetic research
  • Plant diversity and evolution
  • Species identification and classification
  • Plant voucher records
  • Molecular records and inventories
  • Target-capture sequencing approaches
  • DNA laboratory work
Research direction

Connecting diversity with environment

Across these research areas, I am interested in how plant genetic and phenotypic diversity interacts with environmental variation, and how this knowledge can contribute to the understanding, evaluation, and improvement of horticultural and specialty crops.
